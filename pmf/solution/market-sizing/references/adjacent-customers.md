# Expand to adjacent customers — after product-market fit

*One-pager, draft. A technique sketch for a later skill; it uses the rings from `market-sizing`.*

**When:** after product-market fit with the beachhead (customers who paid for three cycles, a retention curve that flattens), and when the beachhead's growth slows or its acquisition cost climbs. Before that, every neighbour is a distraction.

**The rule:** change one thing at a time. Either the customer (a neighbouring ring, same product) or the product (a new product, same customer). Never both in one step: that is a new company.

## 1. List the candidates

| Kind | Where they come from | Longevity example |
|---|---|---|
| **The next ring** | the rings in `pmf/market-size.md`, in order | R1: the same person outside the big cities |
| **A side branch** | a second persona the ICP flagged; buyers of a neighbouring product; the professionals who advise your customer | women 38–48 tracking perimenopause (C1's rival ICP); Thriva and Werlabs customers without a wearable; functional-medicine doctors and coaches |
| **A new product, same customer** | what the beachhead asks for next, in support tickets and cancellations | a blood test sold inside the app |

The best evidence is already in your analytics: people outside the ICP who signed up, paid and stayed anyway. Pull them out first and see which ring or branch they belong to.

## 2. Score each one

| Criterion | Question | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Size** | how many, and at what price? | the ring's count from `market-sizing` |
| **Carry-over** | how much of the product, the references and the channel still works for them? | Moore: neighbours who share a buyer, a pain or a channel come cheapest |
| **Product gap** | what must be built, in weeks? | the "what the product must add" column |
| **Pull** | are they already arriving, paying, staying? | own analytics, inbound, the waitlist |
| **Barriers** | do they have a reason to switch, how strong is the habit, will an incumbent fight back? | interviews, the competitive map |

Order by carry-over and pull first, size second. A big ring with a large product gap comes later than a small ring the product already serves.

## 3. For each neighbour, in order

1. **Hypothesis:** "[neighbour] will pay [price] for [product] because [pain]".
2. **Product delta:** the smallest change that makes the product work for them.
3. **Message delta:** a header and a hook for them (`value-proposition`), on its own page variant.
4. **Channel:** where they are (`icp-finder`, C2), which may differ from the beachhead's.
5. **Test:** ten interviews and one page variant with deposits, as for the beachhead. Same yes threshold.
6. **Signal:** expand when the neighbour's three-cycle retention matches the beachhead's; stop when it doesn't after two months.

## Two real sequences (from Gilles's workshop slides)

- **Revolut**, one audience at a time: expats (an account in seconds, no address or credit check) → digital nomads (money anywhere, no hidden fees) → bank account holders (budgeting, card controls) → credit card spenders (single-use cards, cashback) → the crypto-curious (crypto in one click). Each step kept the foundation and added one product piece for one closely related audience.
- **Intercom**, a grid of niches: it started as live chat for customer support, then added features (chatbots, product tours, a help centre, customer analytics) and use cases (product engagement, marketing, lead generation), and now runs one landing page per feature, use case, industry (financial services, healthcare, education, ecommerce) and company size (early stage, small business, enterprise). Each page is a niche with its own message and channel.

## Longevity, illustrative order (pre-PMF, untested)

| Step | Neighbour | Changes | Product must add | Why this order |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The same person across Europe (5.8m beyond the first cities) | customer (place) | lab reports parsed in each language | everything else carries over |
| 2 | Perimenopause trackers, women 38–48 | customer (persona) | cycle and hormone markers | same wearable, same blood test; Oura says 72% of its members are women |
| 3 | A blood test inside the app, for the same customers | product | partner labs or home kits | the beachhead already buys tests; Oura sells one already |
| 4 | Ages 25–34 and 45–64 | customer (age) | content per life stage | needs step 3 for people who don't test yet |
| 5 | Doctors and coaches who advise them | customer (B2B2C) | a practitioner view of several clients | a channel to many end users at once |

Each step keeps one variable fixed. Step 3 changes the product for the same people; steps 4 and 5 change the people for the product that step 3 built.
