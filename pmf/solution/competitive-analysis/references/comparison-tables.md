# Competitor comparison tables: what the good ones share

Drawn from nine comparison tables startups made in Gilles's workshops and pitch decks (ESG reporting, document AI, 3D avatars, crypto compliance, onboarding agents, Web3 loyalty, internal events, ad creative, AI research). They differ in look; they share a structure.

## The structure

1. **Our product is the first column, and it stands out**: a coloured column, a dashed outline or a bold header. The reader finds it before reading a cell.
2. **Five to eight alternatives after it.** The status quo gets a column when it is what people use today ("Human CSM", "consultants", "the spreadsheet").
3. **Fact rows at the top or bottom**, the ones a buyer or investor checks first: founded, funding (amount, round, year), size, HQ or where it is sold, target customer, business model, price.
4. **Six to ten capability rows**, each one concrete thing a buyer looks for, written as the capability itself ("Multiple sizes ad generator", "Self-hosted wallet verification", "Supplier CO2 calculator", "Customer owns the models"). A few name the benefit instead ("Save time and money in corporate events"); those read weaker unless each is something a buyer would say.
5. **Cells you can scan:** ✓ / ✗, a three-level mark (✓, ~ partial, – not available), a traffic light, or a short value ("Medium, 10ct per page", "High: guide steps re-tagged each UI change"). One mark and at most a few words.

## What makes one land, and what doesn't

- **Every row should be a reason someone chooses.** Rows come from what buyers ask (the buyer questions), what they complain about (the complaints that are built into a competitor's model) and what the product promises (the value propositions). A row that only exists because our product has it reads as a sales slide.
- **Rows where everyone has a ✓ waste space** unless the point is "table stakes".
- **Our column shouldn't be all ✓.** A table where we win every row isn't believed. Show the one or two rows where a competitor is ahead.
- **A partial mark needs its reason** ("~ only on its own tests").
- **Sources stay in the full file**, one per cell or per competitor; the slide carries the marks.
- **Our column says what is planned** when the product doesn't exist yet.

## How it relates to the positioning map

The map shows two axes and the free corner. The table shows the same competitors on the concrete things a buyer checks. The map makes the argument; the table backs it up row by row.
