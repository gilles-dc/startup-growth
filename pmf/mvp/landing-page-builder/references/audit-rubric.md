# Audit rubric

Score each section 1–5. Quote the page in every reason. 5 = nothing to change; 3 = works but leaves conversion on the table; 1 = missing or actively hurting.

For every issue, name the force it affects: **desire** (not enough value or intrigue), **labor** (too much to read or process), or **confusion** (unclear what, why, or what to do next).

| Section | 5 looks like | Common failures |
|---|---|---|
| **Five-second test** | A first-time visitor can say what's sold, why it's better, and what to do next. | Can't tell what the product is without scrolling. |
| **Header** | Says exactly what's sold as a customer benefit; specific; one hook (bold specific claim, main objection handled, or persona). Passes "read only this line". | Slogan ("Supercharge your workflow"), category jargon, self-congratulation ("The #1 platform"), capability instead of benefit, "AI-powered" as the value. |
| **Subheader** | One or two sentences: introduces the product and how it creates the header's value; names the feature that makes the claim believable. | Repeats the header; a list of features; too long; missing. |
| **Hero image** | The product itself, in action, reinforcing the header. | Stock photo, abstract illustration, unreadable dashboard, decoration. |
| **Social proof** | Logos/testimonials of credible customers vouching for the hero's value, right below the hero. | Missing; generic testimonials with no name or role; logos without permission; too far down the page. |
| **CTA** | A specific verb continuing the value prop, visible above the fold and repeated after the features; short incentive; low friction. | "Submit", "Learn more", "Contact us"; competing CTAs; only at the bottom; a long form for a first step. |
| **Features & objections** | 3–6 features, each a blunt value-prop header + short copy on how the status quo falls short and how this is better + an image of the feature in action; each ties back to the hero; the main objections are handled. | Feature names instead of benefits; walls of text; no objections handled; eye-candy icons; features unrelated to the hero promise. |
| **Remaining questions** | Pricing, how it works, security/trust, how to start: answered before the final CTA. | Visitor still asks "how much?", "is it safe?", "how do I start?". |
| **Information density** | High ratio of ideas to elements; every word and visual earns its place. | Repetition, filler sections, decorative copy. |
| **Design** | Easy to skim, clearly structured, looks put together, reflects the brand. | Clutter, inconsistent styles, low contrast, slow load, broken on mobile. |
| **Consistency** | The same promise from header to features to CTA; matches the ad or source that brought the visitor. | Header promises one thing, features sell another; ad and page don't match. |
| **Honesty** | Every claim true and supportable; validation pages honest about what exists today. | Invented numbers, fake logos, unverifiable superlatives. |
