# The landing page method

## 1. What a landing page does

Acquisition gets potential customers to the page. **Conversion** entices them with value propositions so they want the product.

Explain product features **as customer benefits**: product on one side, market on the other.

### Page types

| Type | Products | Personas |
|---|---|---|
| Product landing page | 1 | several |
| Audience landing page | several | 1 |
| Catch-all landing page (homepage) | several | several |

Audience pages can be cut by use case, industry or company size. All three types use the same template. The further a page departs from it, the more confused the average visitor.

### Conversion

Conversion rate = signups / visitors.

CTAs sit on a spectrum from marketing to product:
- marketing: "get on wait list", "early access", "sign up for beta", "request demo"
- product: "sign up", "get started"
- best: a specific verb that continues the value prop: "Plan your music event", "Generate ideas", "Build your store", "Audit my business", "Boost your revenue", "Invest now", "Link your assets"

### The formula

**Conversion = desire − (labor + confusion)**

- **+ Desire:** entice with value, create intrigue. Make it as easy as possible for visitors to imagine how their lives will be better with the product in it.
- **− Labor:** minimise the work visitors do to get the value props. The more words and images to process, the more tiring. Every word, visual and element should add meaning.
- **− Confusion:** make it crystal clear how the product improves the visitor's life. Be specific and consistent at every step.

### Messaging > design

1. Hone the message: identify the most desirable value propositions.
2. Combine copy and media to convey them as clearly and concisely as possible.
3. Design the page so it enhances the clarity and impact of step 2.

## 2. Value propositions

A value proposition matches product quality to customer benefit.
- Product quality: "Here's what our product can do."
- Value prop: "Here's what **you** can do with our product."

**One quality, several benefits.** Imagine the product is fast:
| benefit | value prop |
|---|---|
| quicker output | "Get work done faster." |
| greater output | "Get more work done." |
| more efficiency | "Save yourself time." |

Imagine it's secure:
| benefit | value prop |
|---|---|
| protection | "If you lose your card, freeze it from the app." |
| control | "Turn off contactless, online payments and withdrawals from the app." |

### Generating value props

1. What bad alternatives do people resort to without your product?
2. How is your product better than that alternative?
3. Write it as an action statement: that's the value prop.

**Duolingo**
| Bad alternative | How you're better | Value prop |
|---|---|---|
| Expensive language courses and subscriptions | 100% free language learning | Learn a new language for free. |
| In-person courses with long commitments | Flexible learning at your own pace | Learn a new language whenever, wherever. |
| Boring language courses | Real-world conversations in new languages | Learn a language through real-world communication. |

**Revolut**
| Bad alternative | Better solution | Value prop |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction fees: moving money costs money | No fees | Spend, withdraw and send money anywhere in the world. No additional fees. |
| Transaction delays: wastes productivity, hinders opportunity | Money arrives right away | Send and get money in real time, to and from anywhere in the world. |
| Limited control: hard to budget, you lose money | Limits, analytics, notifications | Take control of your money with spending notifications and smart budgeting tools. |
| Cumbersome to open an account, not available for everyone | Semi-automated KYC in 5 minutes | Open an account in seconds without street address or credit checks. |

## 3. The proven template

1. **NAVBAR** — top of the page: company logo and site links.
2. **HERO** — big section with header, subheader and the most enticing imagery.
3. **SOCIAL PROOF** — logos and/or testimonials of the best-known clients.
4. **CALL-TO-ACTION** — signup button with a concise incentive.
5. **FEATURES** (& objections) — key value propositions: why people would choose you.
6. **CALL-TO-ACTION ↺** — repetition cooks an idea into people's heads.
7. **FOOTER** — miscellaneous links.

Above the fold: navbar, hero, social proof, CTA. **Above the fold you earn the attention. Below the fold you earn the sale.**

Above-the-fold sequence:
1. Your magic (header)
2. How you create magic (subheader)
3. Visualize the magic (image)
4. They say it's magic (social proof)
5. Click here for magic (CTA)

## 4. Hero

**If the visitor reads nothing else on the page, they should still get the product and why they should use it.**

### Header

Descriptive, while concise. Hook them, or lose them for good. Be specific; don't be vague ("Improve your workflow!"). If a visitor reads only this, would they know what you're selling? Make them want to scroll, but don't require them to scroll.

How to write killer headers:
1. Identify how users get value from the product (value props, section 2).
2. Add a hook to keep them reading: **boldly address likely scepticism**, or make a bold, specific claim.
3. Speak directly to the customer persona.

**Hook: address the main objection.** "Build your own website." triggers:
- "But I don't know how to code."
- "This'll take too long. I'm not a designer."
- "This will be limited like other such tools."

| Objection | Updated header |
|---|---|
| Don't know how to code | Build your own website. Without code. |
| It'll take too long | Build your own website in 20 minutes. No code. |
| Low functionality | Build a custom website in 20 minutes. No code. |

| Value prop | Objection | Value + hook |
|---|---|---|
| Rank higher on Google | "I suck at SEO." | You don't have to be an SEO pro to rank higher on Google. |
| Ace the SAT | "I bet it's hard work." | Ace the SAT with just 10 minutes of studying per day. |
| ED pills | "I don't want to ask my doctor." | Erectile dysfunction pills. Delivered to your door. |

| Product | Original | Objection | Updated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Airbnb | Experience new cities like a local. | Only for long stays | Experience new cities like a local. No minimum stays. |
| Dropbox | Upload your files to the cloud automatically. | Security and privacy risks | … Chosen by Fortune 500s for superior security. |
| Deliveroo | Get your favourite meals in one click. | High delivery costs | … No extra fees. |
| Webflow | Launch your website yourself. | I can't code | Build your own website in 15 minutes. No code. |
| Robinhood | Trade stocks for free. | There's a minimum size | Stock trading without fees. No minimums. |
| Slack | Communicate effectively with everyone in one place. | It'll be expensive | Communicate with everyone in one place. Free for teams. |

Find objections by asking customers: "What almost stopped you from buying?" and "Why do you think others haven't bought from us yet?" Address the **major** objection in the header; the rest go in the features.

**Hook: speak to the persona.**
| Product | Persona | Header |
|---|---|---|
| Deliveroo | Young working professionals | Get your favourite local meals right to your office. No delivery fees. |
| Deliveroo | Elderly without transportation | Get contactless delivery to your home. No delivery fees. |
| Revolut | Teens, young adults without credit | Get paid back by your friends instantly. No fees. |
| Revolut | Small business owners | Reimburse your employees instantly. No fees. |
| Robinhood | Young, upper-middle-class millennials | Get started creating your stock portfolio. No minimums. |
| Robinhood | Retired professionals | Earn dividends on your retirement savings. Start investing with no minimums. |
| Slack | Small business owners | Keep your business comms organised. For free. |
| Slack | College organisations | Build your student organisation in one communication channel. For free. |

Several personas on one page: **"choose your own adventure"**: let visitors pick their persona at the top and route them to the matching section.

### Subheader

Once visitors understand **what** the product does, the subheader specifies **how**. When you promise amazing value, visitors think "OK, but how?":
- How does the product work, exactly?
- Which feature makes the claim believable?

Structure: header = 1. value prop · subheader = 2. introduce the product + 3. how it creates the value prop. One or two sentences.

| Header | Subheader |
|---|---|
| How small brands sell more online | Privy's conversion, email marketing and text messaging tools help you get more customers from your traffic. |
| Your most restful sleep is just a sip away | Calming teas that help you relax, unwind and drift into a deep, restorative sleep. |
| Ace the SAT with just 10 minutes of studying a day | 10-minute microlessons designed to boost confidence and make SAT strategies easy to remember. |
| Experience new cities like a local. No minimum stays. | An online rental marketplace with thousands of options in your area. |
| Get paid back by your friends instantly. No fees. | Revolut is a money transfer platform that instantly connects you and your friends' banks. |
| Build your own website in 15 minutes. No code. | Webflow is a website building platform that uses plug-and-play tech to help you launch quickly. |
| Stock trading without fees. No minimums. | Robinhood is an e-trading platform that allows instant investments without trading fees. |
| Keep your business communication organised in one place. For free. | Slack is an all-in-one communication platform that organises your conversations into channels, threads and direct messages. |

### Imagery

Complement and reinforce the copy; don't distract from it. Show the product, in action if possible. Visualise the value props in the header and subheader. Physical products: a picture. Software: a screenshot.

"In advertising, the greatest thing to be achieved is believability. Nothing is more believable than the product itself." — Leo Burnett

## 5. Social proof

When visitors scroll down from the value props in the hero, they should see other people and companies, not you, vouching for those value props: **logos** and **testimonials**. Make them feel as if everyone is already using the product, except them. Create FOMO.

Super-early stage, nobody using it yet? Be resourceful and play the hand you're dealt:
- give the product to people at well-known companies; if they use it, ask to show their logo
- pilot users, design partners, advisors, accelerator or programme logos, press, community size, waitlist size
- a founder's credible background, stated plainly
- while you build the page, an **illustrative testimonial**: an imaginary customer who fits the ICP, saying in the customer's own words from the research what they can finally do. Mark it as an example, and replace it with a real quote from an interview before real visitors see the page. It is the quote your interviews have to find.

Never invent customers, logos or numbers, and never show a made-up testimonial to real visitors.

## 6. Call-to-action

Get visitors to act on their excitement and FOMO. Make it feel easy. A button or a short form. The CTA is the next step that fulfils the header's claim: "click here for magic".

| Value prop | CTA |
|---|---|
| Record podcasts in studio quality | Start recording |
| Learn why users leave your site | Show my heatmap |
| Order from your favourite local restaurants | Find food |
| Get paid back by friends instantly | Set up your account |
| Experience your getaway like a real local | Find a rental |
| Protect your investments from hacks | Secure my assets |
| Turn young audiences into lifelong fans with digital collectibles | Build my NFT campaign |

Weak: a vague request for a meeting with no product context. Repeat the CTA after the features.

## 7. Features & objections

Put all value props on the table and proactively address objections visitors might have. Make the overarching hero value prop concrete, feature by feature. Usually 3–6 features.

Examples:
- *Cook the perfect steak, every time:* Cooks and sears · No clean up · More than just meat
- *Invest in fair agro-trade with peace of mind:* Earn fixed 8% APR · Verify every step on-chain · Audited smart contracts
- *Podcasts that look and sound amazing:* Local recordings in 4K quality · Uncompressed crystal-clear audio

Objections as features:
- "… but it'll be complicated" → **So simple Grandma can use it.** Guests join with one click. No downloads, no accounts. We made this for non-techies.
- "… but I can't edit" → **Everyone can edit with our Magic Editor.** Save hours of editing with a few clicks: upload your logo, change the background, choose your speaker layout, cut clips, export.

A "How it works" section (3 steps) is a common and effective feature block.

Each feature: **header · paragraph · imagery.**

**Feature copy:** concisely describe the value of the feature and address possible objections. Point out how the status quo falls short and describe how you make it better. Short, simple, specific. No sales fluff. Bullet points are fine.

| Header | Paragraph |
|---|---|
| A simpler workflow for docs & tasks | Tired of linking Google Docs and Trello? Notion blends the two. Full-powered project trackers with docs inside. Save your team from context switching. |
| Invest for free | We've cut the fat that makes other brokerages costly, like manual account management and hundreds of storefront locations, so we can offer zero-commission trading. |
| Truly private messaging | Status uses the peer-to-peer protocol Whisper and end-to-end encryption to protect your communication from third parties. Only you can view your messages. |
| With LOOP, parents are more connected with each other. | • Create a chat room to organise school carpools. • Find a trusted babysitter on the fly. • Arrange playdates and (kid-free) social gatherings. |

**Feature imagery:** product screenshot or mockup, or an image that shows the feature in action. It must add to the story; avoid meaningless eye candy. Animated GIFs work well.

Before the final CTA, ask: **"What questions could possibly be left?"** Answer them (FAQ, pricing clarity, security, how to start).

## 8. Navbar and footer

Logo · links to key sections · CTA.

## 9. Information density and design

**Information density:** use copy and visuals to say a lot with a little: a high ratio of ideas to elements. This raises desire and lowers labor and confusion together.

**Design:** a visually appealing design helps conversion by showing you're not an amateur. Don't overdo it, but make sure:
- it's enjoyable to skim
- it looks thoughtfully put together
- each section is clearly structured
- it reflects what you stand for (brand)

## 10. Getting feedback

**People outside the target audience:** how enticing and understandable is the copy to people unfamiliar with the market or product? Many over-assume the audience's baseline knowledge.
**People from the target audience:** is the messaging unique and descriptive enough to choose you over the alternatives?

Questions:
- Would you take out your credit card and sign up right now? If not, what would it take?
- Rate how well the page sustained your interest, 1–10.
- What would you rewrite or redesign so it better sustains your interest?
- What unanswered questions are you left with?
- Something awesome you wanted more detail on?
- If you had to delete half the page's imagery and copy, which would it be?
- What triggered your "bulls*t" reflex?
